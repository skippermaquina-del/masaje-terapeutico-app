# Muscles of the Hip & Thigh

## Hip Flexors

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Psoas major | Transverse processes/bodies of T12–L5 | Lesser trochanter of femur | Flexes the hip (and spine bilaterally); laterally rotates the hip |
| Iliacus | Iliac fossa | Lesser trochanter of femur | Flexes the hip |
| Iliopsoas | (psoas major + iliacus combined) | Lesser trochanter | Primary hip flexor group — often referred to as one functional unit |

## Gluteal Muscles

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gluteus maximus | Posterior sacrum, coccyx, posterior iliac crest | Gluteal tuberosity + iliotibial band | Extends, laterally rotates, and abducts the hip — the primary hip extensor, especially forceful movements (climbing stairs, standing from a squat) |
| Gluteus medius | Superior gluteal line of ilium | Greater trochanter | Abducts and medially rotates the hip; critical for pelvic stability during walking |
| Gluteus minimus | Inferior gluteal line of ilium | Greater trochanter | Abducts and medially rotates the hip |
| Tensor fasciae latae (TFL) | Anterior iliac crest | Iliotibial (IT) band | Abducts, flexes, and medially rotates the hip |

**Clinical note**: gluteus medius and minimus are essential hip
stabilizers when standing on one leg (like during walking) — weakness
here is a common cause of a hip "drop" or compensatory limp.

## The Deep Six Hip (Lateral) Rotators
These small muscles sit deep to the gluteals and all share the same
general action: laterally rotating the hip.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Piriformis | Anterior sacrum | Greater trochanter | Laterally rotates the hip (also abducts when hip is flexed) |
| Quadratus femoris | Ischial tuberosity | Intertrochanteric crest | Laterally rotates the hip |
| Obturator internus | Obturator foramen (internal surface) | Greater trochanter | Laterally rotates the hip |
| Obturator externus | Obturator foramen (external surface) | Trochanteric fossa | Laterally rotates the hip |
| Gemellus superior | Ischial spine | Greater trochanter | Laterally rotates the hip |
| Gemellus inferior | Ischial tuberosity | Greater trochanter | Laterally rotates the hip |

**Why this group matters clinically**: the **sciatic nerve** runs
through or directly beneath the piriformis in most people. When
piriformis is tight or in spasm, it can compress the sciatic nerve and
mimic sciatica symptoms (pain radiating down the leg) — this is called
piriformis syndrome.

## Adductor Group (Inner Thigh)

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adductor longus | Pubic tubercle | Linea aspera (femur) | Adducts and flexes the hip, medially rotates |
| Adductor magnus | Ischial tuberosity, pubic/ischial rami | Linea aspera, adductor tubercle | Adducts, flexes, extends the hip (largest adductor, has both anterior and posterior fibers) |
| Adductor brevis | Inferior pubic ramus | Linea aspera | Adducts and medially rotates the hip |
| Pectineus | Pectineal line of pubis | Pectineal line of femur | Adducts and flexes the hip |
| Gracilis | Inferior pubic ramus | Medial proximal tibia (pes anserine) | Adducts and flexes the hip; also flexes and medially rotates the knee (crosses two joints) |

## Anterior/Lateral Thigh: Sartorius & Quadriceps

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sartorius | Anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) | Medial proximal tibia (pes anserine) | Flexes, abducts, and laterally rotates the hip; flexes the knee — the longest muscle in the body, crosses two joints |
| Rectus femoris | Anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) | Tibial tuberosity | Flexes the hip AND extends the knee (the only quad muscle that crosses the hip) |
| Vastus lateralis | Linea aspera, gluteal tuberosity | Tibial tuberosity | Extends the knee only |
| Vastus medialis | Linea aspera | Tibial tuberosity | Extends the knee only |
| Vastus intermedius | Anterior/lateral femoral shaft | Tibial tuberosity | Extends the knee only |

**"Pes anserine" note**: sartorius, gracilis, and semitendinosus (a
hamstring) all insert together at the medial proximal tibia, forming
the **pes anserine** ("goose's foot") tendon group — a common memory aid
is "Say Grace before Tea" (Sartorius, Gracilis, semitendinosus).

## Posterior Thigh: Hamstrings

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biceps femoris | Ischial tuberosity (+ femur for short head) | Fibular head | Flexes the knee, extends the hip, laterally rotates the flexed knee |
| Semitendinosus | Ischial tuberosity | Medial proximal tibia (pes anserine) | Flexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed knee |
| Semimembranosus | Ischial tuberosity | Medial tibial condyle | Flexes the knee, extends the hip, medially rotates the flexed knee |

**Agonist/antagonist pattern**: the hamstrings (knee flexors, hip
extensors) and the quadriceps (knee extensors, and rectus femoris also
a hip flexor) are the classic antagonist pair of the thigh — most
exam questions about pelvic tilt and knee mechanics come back to the
balance between these two groups.

## Pelvic Tilt — Putting It Together
- **Anterior pelvic tilt** (low back sways forward): typically involves
  tight hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) and weak hip extensors
  (gluteus maximus, hamstrings).
- **Posterior pelvic tilt**: typically the reverse pattern — tight
  hamstrings/glutes, weaker hip flexors.
