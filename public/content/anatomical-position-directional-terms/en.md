# Anatomical Position & Directional Terms

## The Anatomical Position
All directional terms in anatomy are defined relative to one reference
posture — the **anatomical position**:

- Standing upright
- Feet flat on the floor, facing forward
- Arms at the sides
- Palms, face, and eyes facing forward (forearms supinated)

Every "anterior," "medial," "superior," etc. you'll ever use assumes the
body is in this position — even when describing a client lying prone or
supine on the table.

## Forearm Positions (worth knowing on sight)
- **Supinated**: palm faces up/forward; radius and ulna are parallel.
- **Pronated**: palm faces down/backward; radius and ulna are crossed.

## The Three Planes of Motion
Planes are imaginary flat surfaces used to describe location and
movement.

- **Sagittal plane**: divides the body into left and right. The
  *midsagittal (median) plane* splits the body into exactly equal left
  and right halves. Movements in this plane: flexion, extension,
  dorsiflexion, plantarflexion.
- **Frontal (coronal) plane**: divides the body into anterior (front) and
  posterior (back) portions. Movements in this plane: abduction,
  adduction, elevation, depression.
- **Transverse (horizontal) plane**: divides the body into superior
  (upper) and inferior (lower) portions. Movement in this plane:
  rotation (medial, lateral, trunk).

## The Two Main Body Cavities
- **Ventral cavity** (anterior): contains the thoracic cavity (heart,
  lungs) and the abdominopelvic cavity (abdominal + pelvic organs),
  separated by the diaphragm.
- **Dorsal cavity** (posterior): contains the cranial cavity (brain) and
  the spinal/vertebral canal (spinal cord).

## Directional Terms — Paired Opposites
| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior (ventral) | toward the front | The sternum is anterior to the spine. |
| Posterior (dorsal) | toward the back | The scapula is posterior to the ribs. |
| Superior (cranial/cephalad) | toward the head | The shoulder is superior to the elbow. |
| Inferior (caudal) | toward the feet | The knee is inferior to the hip. |
| Medial | toward the midline | The sternum is medial to the shoulder. |
| Lateral | away from the midline | The ear is lateral to the nose. |
| Proximal | closer to the trunk/root of a limb | The elbow is proximal to the wrist. |
| Distal | farther from the trunk/root of a limb | The fingers are distal to the wrist. |
| Superficial | closer to the skin surface | The skin is superficial to muscle. |
| Deep | farther from the skin surface, closer to the core | Bone is deep to muscle. |
| Ipsilateral | same side of the body | The right hand and right foot are ipsilateral. |
| Contralateral | opposite side of the body | The right hand and left foot are contralateral. |
| Intermediate | between two structures | — |

## Region-Specific Terms
- **Palmar**: referring to the palm of the hand.
- **Plantar**: referring to the sole of the foot.
- **Flexor surface**: the side of a limb where flexion occurs.
- **Extensor surface**: the side of a limb where extension occurs.

## Why This Matters Clinically
Directional terms let you communicate precisely and consistently with
other professionals — in SOAP notes, referrals, or verbal handoffs —
regardless of how the client happens to be positioned on the table at the
time. "Medial to the greater trochanter" means the same thing whether the
client is prone, supine, or side-lying.
