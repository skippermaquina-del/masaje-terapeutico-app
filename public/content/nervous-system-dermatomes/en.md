# Nervous System & Dermatomes

## Two Major Divisions
- **Central nervous system (CNS)**: the brain and spinal cord — the
  body's control center.
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS)**: all the nerves outside the CNS,
  connecting the CNS to the rest of the body.

## Within the PNS: Two Functional Systems
- **Somatic nervous system**: controls voluntary movement (skeletal
  muscle) and carries conscious sensory information.
- **Autonomic nervous system**: controls involuntary functions (heart
  rate, digestion, gland activity). It has two opposing branches:
  - **Sympathetic**: the "fight or flight" branch — activated by stress,
    danger, or vigorous stimulation. Increases heart rate, redirects
    blood to muscles, heightens alertness.
  - **Parasympathetic**: the "rest and digest" branch — activated by
    calm, safety, and slow rhythmic input. Decreases heart rate, aids
    digestion, promotes relaxation.
- **Massage connection**: slow, rhythmic, light techniques (like
  effleurage or vibration) tend to shift clients toward parasympathetic
  dominance (relaxation); fast, stimulating techniques (like tapotement)
  tend to shift toward sympathetic activation (alertness/energy).

## Neurons
- **Neuron**: the basic nerve cell that transmits electrical signals.
- **Sensory (afferent) neuron**: carries information *from* the body
  *toward* the CNS (input — e.g., "this is hot").
- **Motor (efferent) neuron**: carries commands *from* the CNS *to* the
  body (output — e.g., "contract this muscle").
- **Reflex**: an automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus that
  doesn't require conscious brain processing (e.g., pulling your hand
  away from something hot).

## Sensory Receptors Relevant to Touch
- **Mechanoreceptors**: respond to physical pressure, vibration, or
  displacement of the skin — this is the main receptor category massage
  stimulates.
- **Nociceptors**: detect pain.
- **Proprioceptors**: sense body position and movement (covered in more
  depth in the muscle function topic — includes muscle spindles and
  Golgi tendon organs).

## Dermatomes
- **Dermatome**: a band-like area of skin supplied by sensory fibers
  from a single spinal nerve root.
- Because each dermatome maps to one spinal nerve level, dermatome charts
  are used clinically to help localize a possible nerve root problem —
  for example, numbness or tingling following a specific dermatome
  pattern down the arm can point toward a specific cervical nerve level
  (rather than a purely muscular issue).
- Dermatome patterns run in fairly predictable bands down the limbs and
  around the trunk, which is why nerve-related referral pain often
  follows a straight strip rather than a vague, spread-out area.
- Recognizing "this pain follows a dermatome pattern" is a useful red
  flag: it suggests a possible nerve-root issue, which may be outside a
  massage therapist's scope to treat directly and could warrant a
  referral.

## Why This Matters Clinically
- Understanding sympathetic vs. parasympathetic activation explains why
  massage *technique choice* (not just the fact that you're touching
  someone) determines whether a session feels calming or invigorating.
- Recognizing dermatome-pattern symptoms (vs. vague, non-specific
  discomfort) helps a therapist decide when something may be neurological
  in origin and worth a referral, rather than a straightforward muscular
  issue to work on directly.
