# Body Movements & Joint Actions

All body movements are described starting from the anatomical position.
Knowing these terms cold lets you follow (and answer) any question about
muscle actions, since every muscle action table is just a list of which
movement(s) a muscle produces.

## Sagittal-Plane Movements
- **Flexion**: decreases the angle of a joint.
- **Extension**: increases the angle of a joint (returns toward
  anatomical position).
- **Hyperextension**: extension carried beyond the normal anatomical
  range.
- **Dorsiflexion**: pulling the toes/foot up toward the shin (ankle).
- **Plantarflexion**: pointing the toes/foot downward (ankle) — think "P"
  for point and "P" for plantarflexion.

## Frontal (Coronal)-Plane Movements
- **Abduction**: movement away from the midline.
- **Adduction**: movement toward the midline.
- **Elevation**: raising a structure (e.g., the shoulders, the scapula,
  the mandible).
- **Depression**: lowering a structure.
- **Lateral flexion**: bending the neck or spine sideways, away from
  midline.

## Transverse-Plane Movements
- **Medial (internal) rotation**: rotating toward the midline.
- **Lateral (external) rotation**: rotating away from the midline.
- **Pronation**: forearm rotates so the palm faces down/back (can also
  describe the foot rolling toward the arch).
- **Supination**: forearm rotates so the palm faces up/forward.
- **Inversion**: sole of the foot turns inward, toward the midline.
- **Eversion**: sole of the foot turns outward, away from the midline.

## Multi-Plane / Special Movements
- **Circumduction**: a cone-shaped combination of flexion, abduction,
  extension, and adduction in sequence — used at ball-and-socket and
  condyloid joints (shoulder, hip, wrist, fingers).
- **Protraction**: moving a structure forward/away from the body midline
  (e.g., scapula sliding forward around the rib cage, or the jaw jutting
  forward). Also called scapular abduction for the shoulder blade.
- **Retraction**: moving a structure backward/toward the spine (e.g.,
  squeezing the shoulder blades together). Also called scapular
  adduction.
- **Scapular upward/downward rotation**: the scapula rotating so the
  glenoid cavity faces up (upward rotation, happens during arm-raising)
  or down (downward rotation).

## Quick Reference by Body Region
| Region | Movements Available |
|---|---|
| Shoulder (glenohumeral) | Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, medial/lateral rotation, circumduction, horizontal abduction/adduction |
| Scapula | Elevation, depression, protraction, retraction, upward/downward rotation |
| Elbow | Flexion, extension (hinge joint — one plane only) |
| Forearm | Pronation, supination |
| Wrist | Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, circumduction |
| Hip | Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, medial/lateral rotation, circumduction |
| Knee | Flexion, extension (mostly hinge; slight rotation when flexed) |
| Ankle | Dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion |
| Spine/Neck | Flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation |
| Mandible (jaw) | Elevation, depression, protraction, retraction |

## Why This Matters
Muscle-action tables (which you'll study by body region) are built almost
entirely from this vocabulary — "flexes the elbow," "abducts the hip,"
"laterally rotates the shoulder." If a movement term is unclear, the
muscle action attached to it will be unclear too. Master this list before
moving on to specific muscles.
