# Circulation & the Lymphatic System

## Why Massage Therapists Study Circulation
Massage — especially effleurage and petrissage — mechanically assists
both blood and lymph flow. Understanding these two systems explains *why*
stroke direction matters and *why* certain conditions (like DVT or
lymphedema) are contraindications.

## The Cardiovascular System — Core Vocabulary
- **Artery**: carries blood *away* from the heart (usually oxygenated,
  except the pulmonary artery).
- **Vein**: carries blood *toward* the heart (usually deoxygenated,
  except the pulmonary vein). Veins have valves to prevent backflow —
  this is part of why massage strokes toward the heart assist venous
  return.
- **Capillary**: the smallest vessels, where oxygen/nutrient/waste
  exchange with tissue actually happens.
- **Oxygenated blood**: blood rich in oxygen (bright red).
- **Deoxygenated blood**: blood low in oxygen (darker red).
- **Heart**: the pump driving the whole system.

## Two Circulation Loops
- **Pulmonary circulation**: the short loop between the heart and the
  lungs, where blood picks up oxygen and drops off carbon dioxide.
- **Systemic circulation**: the long loop between the heart and the rest
  of the body, delivering oxygenated blood and returning deoxygenated
  blood.

## The Lymphatic System
- **Lymph**: a clear fluid that originates from blood plasma leaking
  into tissue spaces; it carries waste products, excess fluid, and
  immune cells.
- **Lymphatic vessels**: transport lymph, similar in some ways to veins,
  moving fluid *toward* the heart. Unlike blood, lymph has no central
  pump — it relies on muscle contraction, breathing, and (in a clinical
  setting) manual techniques to move.
- **Lymph nodes**: small structures that filter lymph and are packed
  with immune cells, helping fight infection.
- **Edema**: excess fluid accumulation in tissue — can result from
  impaired venous or lymphatic return.
- **Lymphedema**: a more serious, chronic form of localized swelling
  caused by lymphatic system dysfunction or blockage; it requires
  specialized manual lymphatic drainage technique, not general massage.

## How Massage Direction Connects to These Systems
- Strokes directed **toward the heart (centripetal)** assist venous
  return and lymphatic flow, since neither system has strong forward
  pressure of its own in the limbs.
- Very light pressure is used for lymphatic work, because lymphatic
  vessels sit just under the skin and collapse under too much pressure.
- Firmer pressure can be used for general venous-assist strokes, but
  always directed toward the heart when working over major veins.

## Clinical Connections
- **DVT (deep vein thrombosis)**: a blood clot in a deep vein, most often
  in the leg. It's an absolute contraindication because mechanical
  pressure could dislodge the clot and send it toward the lungs
  (pulmonary embolism) — a medical emergency.
- **Varicosities (varicose veins)**: veins with damaged/weakened valves
  that bulge and can be fragile; deep or vigorous work over them should
  be avoided or heavily modified.
- **Hypertension (high blood pressure)**: when uncontrolled, it's a
  caution for techniques that significantly increase circulation or
  stress the cardiovascular system (like vigorous petrissage or
  compression).
