# Pathology & Contraindications

## Basic Pathology Vocabulary
- **Pathology**: the study of disease.
- **Etiology**: the cause or origin of a disease.
- **Sign**: objective, observable evidence of a condition (something the
  therapist can see or measure).
- **Symptom**: a subjective complaint reported by the client (something
  only they can feel/describe).
- **Acute**: sudden onset, short duration.
- **Chronic**: long-lasting, ongoing.
- **Inflammation**: the body's protective response to injury (heat,
  redness, swelling, pain, loss of function).
- **Infection**: invasion of the body by pathogens (bacteria, viruses,
  fungi).
- **Edema**: excess fluid accumulation in tissue.
- **Autoimmune disease**: a condition where the immune system mistakenly
  attacks the body's own healthy tissue.

## Local vs. Systemic Contraindications
- A **local contraindication** means a specific area should be avoided,
  but the rest of the session can proceed (e.g., a bruise, a healing
  scar, a varicose vein).
- A **systemic (absolute) contraindication** means the *entire session*
  should not happen, and often requires medical clearance before any
  massage is given (e.g., a fever, an active infection, a blood clot).

## High-Yield Absolute (Systemic) Contraindications
Massage should **not** be performed, and physician clearance is generally
required, when any of these are present:

- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or suspected blood clot
- Fever
- Sepsis or active systemic infection
- Active, untreated tuberculosis
- Recent heart attack (myocardial infarction) or stroke
- Unhealed fractures

## Common Conditions and How They're Handled

| Condition | Typical Massage Status | Physician Clearance |
|---|---|---|
| Fibromyalgia | Indicated (often benefits from gentle work) | Not usually required |
| Osteoarthritis | Indicated, adapt pressure to comfort | Not usually required |
| Osteoporosis | Gentle pressure only, avoid strong compression | Not usually required |
| Rheumatoid arthritis (active flare) | Avoid the inflamed joint locally | Recommended |
| Multiple sclerosis | Indicated with caution (fatigue, sensation changes) | Recommended |
| Parkinson's disease | Indicated, can aid relaxation and mobility | Recommended |
| Hypertension (controlled) | Indicated | Not usually required |
| Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) | **Contraindicated** — do not massage the limb | Required |
| Diabetes (controlled) | Indicated, monitor skin/sensation | Not usually required |
| Hepatitis (active) | **Contraindicated** | Required |
| Psoriasis | Avoid open/active lesions, otherwise indicated | Not usually required |
| Cellulitis | **Contraindicated** (active bacterial skin infection) | Required |
| Lymphedema | Specialized technique only, not general massage | Required |
| MRSA | **Contraindicated** (contagious, resistant infection) | Required |
| Fever | **Contraindicated** (systemic sign of illness) | Required |

## Why These Rules Exist
- Conditions that involve a **clot risk** (DVT) are contraindicated because
  mechanical pressure could dislodge the clot, causing a life-threatening
  embolism.
- **Active infections** (fever, cellulitis, MRSA, active hepatitis,
  active TB) are contraindicated because massage can spread the pathogen
  through the bloodstream/lymphatic system or to the therapist.
- **Fragile bone/tissue conditions** (osteoporosis, some autoimmune
  flares) call for lighter pressure, not avoidance, because deep pressure
  risks fracture or tissue damage.
- **Neurological conditions** (MS, Parkinson's) are generally safe but
  need caution because clients may have altered sensation, fatigue, or
  balance issues that change how they should be positioned or how much
  feedback they can reliably give.

## Key Takeaway for the Exam
When you see a pathology question, first ask: *is this a local issue
(avoid the area) or a systemic issue (avoid the session)?* Then ask:
*does this condition involve infection, clotting risk, or fragile
tissue?* Those two questions resolve most contraindication questions
correctly.
