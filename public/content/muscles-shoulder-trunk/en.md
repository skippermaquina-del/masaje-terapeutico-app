# Muscles of the Shoulder Girdle & Trunk

## Two Different Muscle Groups Around the Shoulder
It helps to separate these into two categories:
- **Shoulder girdle muscles**: attach to or move the **scapula and/or
  clavicle** (not the humerus directly).
- **Shoulder joint (glenohumeral) muscles**: move the **humerus** at
  the shoulder joint itself.

## Shoulder Girdle Muscles (Move the Scapula/Clavicle)

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trapezius (upper fibers) | Occipital bone, nuchal ligament | Lateral clavicle, acromion | Extends neck/head, elevates and upwardly rotates scapula |
| Trapezius (middle fibers) | Spinous processes C7–T12 | Scapular spine | Retracts the scapula |
| Trapezius (lower fibers) | Spinous processes T12 area | Scapular spine | Depresses the scapula |
| Rhomboid major/minor | Spinous processes C7–T5 | Medial (vertebral) border of scapula | Retracts and downwardly rotates the scapula |
| Levator scapulae | Transverse processes C1–C4 | Superior angle of scapula | Elevates and downwardly rotates the scapula; laterally flexes the neck |
| Serratus anterior | Ribs 1–8 | Medial border of scapula (underside) | Protracts and upwardly rotates the scapula (keeps scapula flat against the ribs) |
| Pectoralis minor | Ribs 3–5 | Coracoid process of scapula | Depresses and protracts the scapula, downwardly rotates it |

**Clinical note**: a weak serratus anterior is the classic cause of a
"winged scapula" (the shoulder blade sticks out) because it's what
holds the scapula flat against the rib cage.

## Trunk (Core) Muscles

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rectus abdominis | Pubic bone | Ribs 5–7, sternum's xiphoid process | Flexes the spine, compresses the abdomen (the "six-pack" muscle) |
| External oblique | Ribs 5–12 | Iliac crest, abdominal fascia | Unilateral: lateral flexion + rotation. Bilateral: trunk flexion |
| Internal oblique | Iliac crest, lower trunk fascia | Ribs 7–12 | Same general actions as external oblique, deeper layer |
| Transverse abdominis | Ribs 7–12, iliac crest | Abdominal fascia | Compresses the abdominal contents (core stabilizer, minimal direct movement) |
| Erector spinae (group) | Spans the length of the spine, sacrum, ribs | Ribs, vertebrae, skull | Unilateral: lateral flexion. Bilateral: extends the spine and head |
| Quadratus lumborum | Posterior iliac crest | Rib 12, transverse processes L1–L4 | Unilateral: lateral flexion of spine, hikes the hip. Bilateral: extends lumbar spine |

**Memory tip**: quadratus lumborum is sometimes nicknamed the "hip
hiker" because unilateral contraction elevates that side of the pelvis.

## Muscles That Move the Shoulder Joint (Humerus)

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pectoralis major | Clavicle, sternum, ribs 1–8 | Intertubercular groove of humerus | Adducts, medially rotates, and flexes the shoulder |
| Latissimus dorsi | Spine T6–L5, lower ribs, iliac crest | Intertubercular groove of humerus | Extends, adducts, and medially rotates the shoulder |
| Deltoid (anterior/middle/posterior) | Clavicle, acromion, scapular spine | Deltoid tuberosity of humerus | Anterior: flexes/medially rotates. Middle: abducts. Posterior: extends/laterally rotates |
| Teres major | Inferior lateral border of scapula | Intertubercular groove of humerus | Extends, adducts, medially rotates the shoulder |
| Coracobrachialis | Coracoid process of scapula | Medial humeral shaft | Flexes and adducts the shoulder |

## The Rotator Cuff (SITS Muscles)
The rotator cuff is a group of four muscles that stabilize the head of
the humerus in the shallow glenoid cavity, in addition to producing
movement. A common way to remember them is the acronym **SITS**:

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S**upraspinatus | Supraspinous fossa of scapula | Greater tubercle of humerus | Abducts the shoulder; stabilizes the humeral head |
| **I**nfraspinatus | Infraspinous fossa of scapula | Greater tubercle of humerus | Laterally rotates the shoulder |
| **T**eres minor | Superior lateral border of scapula | Greater tubercle of humerus | Laterally rotates the shoulder |
| **S**ubscapularis | Subscapular fossa (front of scapula) | Lesser tubercle of humerus | Medially rotates the shoulder |

**Key distinction**: subscapularis is the only rotator cuff muscle that
medially rotates and the only one that inserts on the *lesser* tubercle
— the other three insert on the *greater* tubercle and all contribute
to lateral rotation or abduction.

## Why This Grouping Matters
Massage therapists frequently treat shoulder-girdle tension (upper
trapezius, levator scapulae) and rotator cuff issues, so keeping these
categories straight — "does this muscle move the scapula, or does it
move the humerus?" — helps you reason through unfamiliar exam questions
even when you can't recall a specific origin/insertion.
