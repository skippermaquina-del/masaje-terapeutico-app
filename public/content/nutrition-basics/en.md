# Nutrition Basics

## Scope Reminder First
Massage therapists provide **basic nutrition information only** — never
personalized diet plans, supplement dosing, or medical nutrition advice.
Specific dietary needs should be referred to a registered dietitian or
the client's physician. Nutrition questions on the exam mostly test
whether you know the six basic nutrients and can recognize when
something is outside scope.

## The Six Basic Nutrients
1. **Water**: involved in essentially every chemical process in the
   body, including energy production. Dehydration causes fatigue. A
   common general guideline is roughly half your body weight in ounces
   per day, but medical conditions (e.g., heart failure) can change that
   — always defer to a physician's guidance for those clients.
2. **Protein**: roughly 12–20% of total diet. Found in meat, poultry,
   beans, legumes. Repairs and rebuilds tissue — it does *not* by itself
   build muscle; exercise (mechanical stress) is what builds muscle,
   protein just supplies the repair material.
3. **Carbohydrates**: roughly 55–60% of total diet (some modern
   guidelines use a wider 45–65% range). The body's primary fuel source
   — the brain alone uses a large share of circulating carbohydrate
   just to function.
   - **Simple carbohydrates** (refined sugars): best kept limited.
   - **Complex carbohydrates** (whole grains, pasta, cereals): provide
     more sustained energy.
   - **High-glycemic foods** raise blood sugar quickly — useful *after*
     exercise to replenish fuel.
   - **Low/mid-glycemic foods** raise blood sugar more slowly — better
     *before* a long bout of exercise, to avoid an energy spike/crash.
4. **Fats**: no more than about 30% of total diet.
   - **Saturated fats**: solid at room temperature, the less healthy
     category; keep to roughly 10% or less of total diet.
   - **Unsaturated fats**: liquid at room temperature (oils); healthier,
     up to about 20% of total diet.
   - **Cholesterol** is a type of fat: **HDL** (high-density lipoprotein)
     is the "good" cholesterol; **LDL** (low-density lipoprotein) is the
     "bad" one.
5. **Vitamins**: no caloric value, but essential for many body
   processes.
   - **Water-soluble** (B and C): excess is excreted in urine — harder
     to overdose.
   - **Fat-soluble** (A, D, E, K): excess is stored in body fat and *can*
     build up to toxic levels over time.
6. **Minerals**: needed only in small (trace) amounts.
   - **Iron**: especially relevant during the menstrual years.
   - **Calcium**: especially relevant during peak bone-growing years and
     after menopause.
   - Supplemental iron or calcium should be taken only under a
     physician's guidance.

## Quick Memory Aids
- One gram of protein or carbohydrate ≈ 4 calories; one gram of fat ≈ 9
  calories (more than double).
- "HDL = Happy" (good cholesterol), "LDL = Lousy" (bad cholesterol).

## Herbs & Supplements — General Guidance
If a client asks about herbs or supplements, remember:
- More is not necessarily better — treat dosing seriously, like
  medication.
- Don't recommend dosages based on anecdote ("ask a friend") — that's
  outside scope and unsafe.
- If something isn't working quickly, the answer isn't "take more" —
  that's a decision for a qualified provider.
- This is squarely outside a massage therapist's scope of practice to
  advise on directly; general awareness is useful, but specific
  recommendations should come from a licensed provider.

## Why This Shows Up on the Exam
Nutrition questions test baseline health literacy, not expertise. The
exam wants to confirm you know general categories (six nutrients, water-
vs. fat-soluble vitamins, macronutrient percentages) and — just as
importantly — that you recognize giving specific nutrition advice is
outside a massage therapist's scope of practice.
