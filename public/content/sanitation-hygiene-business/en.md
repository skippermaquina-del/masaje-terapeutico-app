# Sanitation, Hygiene, Hydrotherapy & Business Practice

## Why Sanitation Is a Legal Requirement, Not Just Good Practice
Cleanliness isn't optional etiquette — it's a legal and ethical
obligation. Improper sanitation puts both the client and therapist at
risk of infection, and can result in licensing consequences.

## Core Sanitation Vocabulary
- **Handwashing**: the single most effective infection-control measure;
  should happen before and after every session.
- **Disinfection**: using chemical agents to kill pathogens on
  surfaces/equipment (tables, face cradles, bolsters).
- **Universal precautions**: treating all bodily fluids as if they were
  potentially infectious, regardless of the client's known health
  status — this protects against unknown/undisclosed conditions.
- **Cross-contamination prevention**: avoiding the spread of pathogens
  between clients (e.g., changing linens between every client, not
  reusing unwashed tools).
- **Laundering protocol**: linens should be laundered after each single
  use, not reused between clients.
- **Gloves**: used when there's a risk of contact with broken skin,
  lesions, or bodily fluids.

## Table & Equipment Setup
- Clean linens for every client, every session — no exceptions.
- Disinfect the table, face cradle, and any tools between clients.
- Personal hygiene (clean hands, trimmed nails, clean clothing) protects
  both the client's experience and their safety.

## Hydrotherapy Basics
Hydrotherapy uses hot and cold application as part of treatment.

- **Heat** generally causes **vasodilation** (blood vessels widen),
  increasing local blood flow — useful for chronic, non-inflamed tissue.
- **Cold** generally causes **vasoconstriction** (blood vessels narrow),
  reducing blood flow and inflammation — useful for acute injury.
- **Key rule: acute inflammation calls for cold, not heat.** Applying
  heat to a fresh, inflamed injury can worsen swelling.
- General effects of heat: increased blood flow, increased pulse rate,
  dilation of peripheral vessels; a *decreased* metabolic rate is **not**
  a typical effect of heat (heat tends to increase metabolic activity
  locally).

## Business & Professional Practice
- **Documentation**: SOAP notes and accurate records (covered in the
  Assessment & Documentation topic) are also a business/legal safeguard.
- **Ethical advertising**: marketing claims must be honest and not
  promise outcomes massage can't reliably deliver (e.g., claiming to
  "cure" a medical condition).
- **Record retention**: client records should be kept for a period set
  by local/state regulation, and stored securely and confidentially.
- **Risk management**: maintaining liability insurance, proper
  documentation, and clear consent processes all reduce professional
  risk.
- **Therapist self-care**: proper body mechanics, workload pacing, and
  personal health protect a therapist's ability to work safely over a
  full career — this is also tested because poor self-care leads to
  injury and shortens a therapist's professional lifespan.

## Common Exam Scenarios
- A client has visible skin lesions: avoid the area, follow infection
  control protocols, don't treat blindly.
- A linen or tool was used with the previous client and hasn't been
  changed/disinfected: this is a sanitation violation regardless of how
  "clean" it looks.
- A client has an acute, swollen injury: use cold, not heat, and modify
  or avoid direct work on the area per contraindication guidelines.
