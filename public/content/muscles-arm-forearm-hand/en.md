# Muscles of the Arm, Forearm & Hand

## Muscles That Move the Elbow (Upper Arm)

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biceps brachii | Two heads: supraglenoid tubercle + coracoid process of scapula | Radial tuberosity | Flexes the elbow, supinates the forearm, assists shoulder flexion |
| Brachialis | Distal anterior humeral shaft | Ulnar tuberosity | Flexes the elbow (pure elbow flexor — no shoulder or forearm action) |
| Brachioradialis | Lateral supracondylar ridge of humerus | Styloid process of radius | Flexes the elbow, especially with the hand in neutral (thumb-up) position |
| Triceps brachii | Three heads: infraglenoid tubercle + two points on humeral shaft | Olecranon process of ulna | Extends the elbow; long head also extends the shoulder |

**Quick logic check**: biceps brachii crosses *two* joints (shoulder and
elbow) and has two separate actions at each; brachialis crosses only the
elbow, making it a "pure" elbow flexor with no other job.

## Muscles That Rotate the Forearm

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supinator | Lateral epicondyle of humerus, proximal ulna | Proximal lateral radius | Supinates the forearm (turns palm up) |
| Pronator teres | Medial epicondyle of humerus, coronoid process of ulna | Lateral proximal radius | Pronates the forearm, assists elbow flexion |
| Pronator quadratus | Distal anterior ulna | Distal anterior radius | Pronates the forearm (the "fine-tuning" pronator, used for small/fast movements) |

**Memory tip**: think of the biceps as "the supinator with a
corkscrew-turning motion" — it's actually the strongest forearm
supinator, more powerful than the supinator muscle itself in most
positions, which is why the biceps is heavily involved when turning a
screwdriver clockwise with the palm-up motion.

## Muscles That Move the Wrist & Fingers

Most anterior forearm muscles are wrist/finger **flexors**, sharing a
common origin at the **medial epicondyle** of the humerus. Most
posterior forearm muscles are wrist/finger **extensors**, sharing a
common origin at the **lateral epicondyle**.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexor carpi radialis | Medial epicondyle | Base of metacarpals 2–3 | Flexes and abducts the wrist |
| Flexor carpi ulnaris | Medial epicondyle | Base of metacarpal 5 | Flexes and adducts the wrist |
| Palmaris longus | Medial epicondyle | Palmar fascia | Flexes the wrist, tenses the palm |
| Flexor digitorum profundus | Proximal anterior ulna | Distal phalanges of fingers 2–5 | Flexes the fingers |
| Extensor carpi radialis (longus/brevis) | Humerus (supracondylar ridge / lateral epicondyle) | Base of metacarpals 2–3 | Extends and abducts the wrist |
| Extensor carpi ulnaris | Lateral epicondyle | Base of metacarpal 5 | Extends and adducts the wrist |
| Extensor digitorum | Lateral epicondyle | Middle phalanges of the 4 fingers | Extends the fingers and wrist |

**Golfer's elbow vs. tennis elbow**: this "flexors = medial, extensors =
lateral" pattern is exactly why golfer's elbow (medial epicondylitis)
irritates the wrist flexor origin, while tennis elbow (lateral
epicondylitis) irritates the wrist extensor origin.

## Thumb Muscles

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexor pollicis longus/brevis | Radius/ulna (longus), trapezium (brevis) | Phalanges of the thumb | Flexes the thumb |
| Extensor pollicis longus/brevis | Ulna/radius, interosseous membrane | Phalanges of the thumb | Extends the thumb |
| Opponens pollicis | Trapezium, transverse carpal ligament | Proximal phalanx of thumb | Opposes the thumb (brings it across the palm to touch other fingers) |

## Why This Region Matters Clinically
Repetitive strain conditions (carpal tunnel syndrome, golfer's/tennis
elbow, "trigger finger") almost all trace back to the flexor or extensor
groups covered here, since these muscles do the small, repeated
movements involved in typing, gripping tools, and manual work. Knowing
which epicondyle a muscle group originates from tells you a lot about
what kind of repetitive-use injury pattern you're looking at.
