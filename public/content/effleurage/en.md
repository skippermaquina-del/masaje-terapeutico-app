# Effleurage

## Definition
Effleurage is a light, gliding stroke used at the beginning and end of a
massage session, and to link other strokes together. The hand(s) mold to the
contour of the body and glide over the skin without attempting to move deep
muscle layers.

## Technique
- Performed with the palm, fingers, or forearm.
- Direction is usually toward the heart (centripetal) to assist venous
  return, though light effleurage can also move away from the heart to
  warm up tissue.
- Pressure ranges from very light (superficial effleurage) to firm
  (deep effleurage), but the stroke always stays smooth and continuous.
- Hands maintain constant contact with the skin throughout the stroke.

## Physiological Effects
- Increases local blood and lymphatic circulation.
- Warms and prepares the tissue for deeper work.
- Stimulates sensory nerve endings, producing a relaxation response.
- Helps the therapist assess tissue tone, temperature, and texture.

## Indications
- Warm-up and closing of a session.
- General relaxation massage.
- Promoting lymphatic drainage (with very light pressure).

## Contraindications
- Acute inflammation or infection at the site.
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or suspected clot — do not massage the limb.
- Open wounds, burns, or skin infections at the site.
- Undiagnosed lumps or swelling.

## Key Terms
- **Centripetal**: moving toward the center (heart).
- **Superficial effleurage**: light pressure, mainly affects skin and
  superficial fascia.
- **Deep effleurage**: firmer pressure, affects muscle layers, always
  performed toward the heart over veins.
