# Muscles of the Leg, Ankle & Foot

## Posterior Leg (Calf) — Plantarflexors

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gastrocnemius | Medial and lateral condyles of the femur | Calcaneus, via the Achilles tendon | Plantarflexes the ankle AND flexes the knee (crosses both joints) |
| Soleus | Posterior fibula and tibia | Calcaneus, via the Achilles tendon | Plantarflexes the ankle only (doesn't cross the knee) |
| Flexor digitorum longus | Posterior tibial shaft | Distal phalanges of toes 2–5 | Flexes toes 2–5, assists plantarflexion |
| Flexor hallucis longus | Posterior fibula | Distal phalanx of the big toe | Flexes the big toe, assists plantarflexion, supports the arch |
| Tibialis posterior | Posterior tibia/fibula, interosseous membrane | Navicular, cuneiforms, metatarsals 2–4 | Inverts the foot and plantarflexes the ankle |

**Key distinction**: gastrocnemius and soleus together form the
"triceps surae" and share the Achilles tendon insertion, but only
gastrocnemius crosses the knee — this is why a bent-knee calf stretch
targets soleus more, while a straight-knee stretch targets
gastrocnemius more.

## Anterior & Lateral Leg — Dorsiflexors & Evertors

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tibialis anterior | Lateral tibia, interosseous membrane | Base of 1st metatarsal, medial cuneiform | Dorsiflexes the ankle and inverts the foot |
| Extensor digitorum longus | Fibula, lateral tibial condyle | Middle/distal phalanges of toes 2–5 | Extends toes 2–5, assists dorsiflexion |
| Extensor hallucis longus | Anterior fibula, interosseous membrane | Distal phalanx of big toe | Extends the big toe, assists dorsiflexion |
| Peroneus (fibularis) longus | Fibular head, proximal fibula | Base of 1st metatarsal, medial cuneiform | Everts the foot, assists plantarflexion |
| Peroneus (fibularis) brevis | Distal fibula | Base of 5th metatarsal | Everts the foot, assists plantarflexion |

**Interesting overlap**: tibialis anterior and peroneus longus actually
insert at nearly the *same* spot (base of the 1st metatarsal /medial
cuneiform) from opposite directions — one inverts, the other everts —
together forming a functional "stirrup" that supports the arch of the
foot.

## Quick Action Summary by Movement
| Movement | Primary Muscles |
|---|---|
| Plantarflexion | Gastrocnemius, soleus (primary); flexor digitorum longus, flexor hallucis longus, tibialis posterior, peroneus longus/brevis (assist) |
| Dorsiflexion | Tibialis anterior (primary); extensor digitorum longus, extensor hallucis longus (assist) |
| Inversion | Tibialis anterior, tibialis posterior |
| Eversion | Peroneus longus, peroneus brevis |

## Clinical Connections
- **"Drop foot"** (inability to dorsiflex, causing the foot to drag)
  points to a problem with **tibialis anterior** or the nerve supplying
  it.
- **Inversion ankle sprains** (the most common type of ankle sprain)
  stretch/damage the lateral ligaments — strengthening the **peroneals
  (evertors)** is typically part of rehab, since they resist the
  inward-rolling motion that caused the injury.
- **Achilles tendon** issues involve both gastrocnemius and soleus,
  since they share that common tendon — assessing knee position (bent
  vs. straight) during testing/stretching helps distinguish which
  muscle is more involved.
