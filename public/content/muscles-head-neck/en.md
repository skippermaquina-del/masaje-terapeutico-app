# Muscles of the Head & Neck

Before diving into individual muscles, review the **Muscle Function &
Connective Tissue** and **Body Movements & Joint Actions** topics —
every row below is built from that vocabulary (origin/insertion,
flexion/extension, etc.).

## Muscles of Facial Expression & Mastication
Facial muscles are unusual: most insert into *skin* rather than bone,
which is what lets them move the face expressively.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Epicranius (frontal/occipital bellies) | Occipital bone | Skin/muscle around the eyebrow | Raises the eyebrow |
| Orbicularis oculi | Frontal and maxillary bones | Skin around the eye | Closes the eyelid |
| Orbicularis oris | Tissue surrounding the mouth | Skin of the lips | Closes/purses the lips |
| Buccinator | Outer surface of maxilla and mandible | Orbicularis oris | Compresses the cheek (as in blowing air) |
| Zygomaticus (major/minor) | Zygomatic bone | Orbicularis oris | Raises the corner of the mouth (smiling) |
| Masseter | Zygomatic arch | Angle and ramus of the mandible | Elevates the mandible (closes the jaw) |
| Temporalis | Temporal bone | Coronoid process of the mandible | Closes the jaw; assists retraction |
| Platysma | Skin over the upper chest/shoulder | Mandible, lower lip, corner of mouth | Depresses the mandible against resistance, tenses neck skin |

**Quick logic check**: masseter and temporalis both close the jaw
(elevate the mandible) — they're synergists for that action, which is
why jaw-clenching tension often shows up in both.

## Muscles That Move the Head & Neck
These muscles connect the skull/cervical spine to the trunk, and move
or stabilize the head and neck.

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Action (unilateral / bilateral) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sternocleidomastoid (SCM) | Sternum (manubrium) + medial clavicle | Mastoid process of the temporal bone | Unilateral: laterally flexes the neck and rotates the head to the *opposite* side. Bilateral: flexes the neck, assists forced inhalation. |
| Splenius capitis | Nuchal ligament, spinous processes C7–T3 | Mastoid process, lateral nuchal line | Unilateral: rotates and laterally flexes the head toward the same side. Bilateral: extends the head. |
| Splenius cervicis | Spinous processes T3–T6 | Transverse processes C1–C3 | Unilateral: rotates and laterally flexes the neck. Bilateral: extends the head. |
| Scalenes (anterior/medius/posterior) | Transverse processes of cervical vertebrae | First or second rib | Unilateral: laterally flexes the neck. Bilateral: elevate the upper ribs during forced inhalation. |

**Why SCM matters clinically**: it's one of the most commonly
palpated/treated neck muscles, and its unilateral action (rotating the
head to the *opposite* side) trips people up — double-check this one
specifically when studying.

## A Note on Mastication
"Mastication" simply means chewing. The primary muscles of mastication
are the **masseter** and **temporalis** (both close the jaw), along with
the medial and lateral pterygoid muscles (which assist in side-to-side
grinding motion and jaw opening/closing).

## Study Strategy for Muscle Tables
1. First learn the **action** — that's what you'll actually use
   clinically and what most exam questions target.
2. Then learn origin/insertion — remember origin is usually the more
   fixed, proximal attachment; insertion is the one that moves.
3. Group muscles by shared action (e.g., "these three all elevate the
   scapula") rather than memorizing each in isolation.
